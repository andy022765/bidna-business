# What you get: a sample deep diagnostic

> **This is a sample. The company is fictional.**
> The roofing company in this document does not exist — neither it, nor its owner, nor its
> competitors, nor any number in its intake. We work under NDA and never publish a client's
> documents, not even anonymized. So this story was built from scratch, on the same method
> and the same questions we use for a real buyer.
>
> What follows are **selected excerpts**, not the whole document. A full report reads like
> a report; an excerpt shows you the move.

**Who is in the example.** A roofing company in Tampa, Florida. Seven years in business,
four crews, two people in the office. Leads come from Google, referrals and two insurance agents.
The owner arrived saying: "We do good work, and they still pick on price."

---

## 1 · Your business DNA

*Your difference in one sentence that works on the page and in a conversation.*

**What we do here.** We don't invent a difference. We find the one you already practice but don't
consider worth mentioning — then we check whether the whole trade says the same thing.

> ### The difference in one sentence
>
> **Before (the owner's words):** "Quality roofing, done right, 7 years' experience, warranty."
>
> **The check:** of five neighbors in the trade, five say "quality," four say "done right,"
> five mention a warranty. That isn't a difference. That is the price of admission.
>
> **Found in the intake, in passing, question 34:** "We don't take the job if the roof has another
> season in it. We say so: wait a year, save the money." In the last two years, nine callers were
> turned away that way (number from the intake; the example is fictional).
>
> **After:** *We tell people "you don't need this yet" — which is why they believe us when we say "you do."*
>
> **Why it survives a conversation.** It can be checked: there are nine people he turned down.
> A difference that can't be checked falls apart on the second question.

## 2 · Your customer's DNA

*A portrait of your buyer in his own words: what he actually pays for, what he fears, what he argues with.*

**What we do here.** Not "male, 35–55, middle income," but the sentences a person says out loud.
What sells is what sounds like his own thought.

> ### What he is actually paying for
>
> A homeowner isn't paying for a roof. He is paying **to stop thinking about it** — and for his
> wife to stop asking when it will be done.
>
> | What he says | What is behind it |
> |---|---|
> | "I just want to know if it's now or if it can wait" | doesn't want to spend, afraid of missing the moment |
> | "You're not going to find something else later, are you?" | afraid of an estimate that grows at the end |
> | "My neighbor had his done and complained afterwards" | isn't looking for the best, he's looking for the safe one |
> | "Will insurance cover this?" | doesn't know the rules and is embarrassed to ask |
>
> ### Three fears, in order
>
> 1. **Being upsold on things he doesn't need.** The main one. The rest are derivatives.
> 2. **Having to start over** if a crew disappears halfway.
> 3. **Making the wrong call** and finding out two years later, through the ceiling.
>
> **What this means for the copy:** the first line on the site should remove the first fear,
> not praise the materials.

## 3 · A map of the leaks

*Where exactly you lose customers — ranked, not listed. And which lever to pull first.*

**What we do here.** We lay out the path a customer walks and mark where he leaves.
The order matters more than completeness: fix the first one, not all of them.

> ### Where the customer is lost, in order
>
> | # | Where | What happens | How it's fixed |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | **A call during work hours** | The owner is on a roof. Nobody picks up. He calls the next name | today, at no cost |
> | 2 | **"How much will it cost?"** | "We'll have to come look." Half of them won't book a visit before a number | in writing, in a day |
> | 3 | **Silence after the inspection** | The estimate is promised "tonight" and arrives three days later | with a rule, not with people |
> | 4 | **The website** | It talks about the company, and answers none of the four fears above | a week of work |
> | 5 | **Reviews** | Eleven of them; the newest is a year and eight months old | with a collection routine |
>
> **The website is not first. It is fourth.** Until the phone gets answered, work on the site
> is water poured into a leaking bucket.

## 4 · A read on your competitors

*What they show, what you don't, and where your blind spot is.*

**What we do here.** We open the neighbors' sites and copy out their promises word for word.
We aren't looking for who is better. We are looking for **which seat is empty**.

> ### What is already taken in this trade
>
> Five roofing companies in the same city were checked (here they are stand-ins — "Competitor A…E").
>
> | Promise | A | B | C | D | E |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | "Quality" and "experience" | ● | ● | ● | ● | ● |
> | A warranty in years | ● | ● | ● | | ● |
> | Works with insurance | ● | | ● | ● | |
> | Free inspection | ● | ● | ● | ● | ● |
> | A fixed price before the work starts | | | | | |
> | **"We'll tell you if you don't need it yet"** | | | | | |
>
> **Two seats are empty**, and both are about trust, not about quality. The second one the owner
> already practices — he simply never counted it as an argument.
>
> **What we don't do:** we don't compare prices and we don't claim any neighbor does bad work.
> We look at what they say, not at what they are.

## 5 · Three places for AI

*Exactly three places where AI gives you money or time, tied to your losses.*

**What we do here.** Three, not a list of fifteen. Each one is tied to a line in the map of leaks —
otherwise it is a daydream about technology.

> ### Three places, tied to leaks 1, 2 and 5
>
> **One — the phone always gets answered.** Closes leak #1. A voice agent picks up while the crew
> is on a roof: gives the range from your own price list, takes the address and the roof type,
> and emails you after every call. It sits on your existing number through call forwarding.
>
> **Two — a range instead of "we have to come look."** Closes leak #2. The conditions that make
> the price this, and the conditions that make it that — written once and identical everywhere:
> on the site, on the phone, in messages.
>
> **Three — the review is asked for on the day the work ends.** Closes leak #5. Not a "please
> review us" campaign, but one message at the moment the man is standing there looking at his new roof.
>
> **What is not on this list:** a website chatbot, analytics, dashboards. None of them is tied
> to any leak of yours.

## 6 · A document in your hands

*All of the above as a finished PDF. It stays yours even if you never work with us again.*

A real buyer's document is longer than this sample: here you see selected excerpts, there you get
all of it — including what we checked and **could not confirm**. Separate sections cover:

- **sources** — where each fact came from: the intake, open sources, or the owner's own words;
- **what we blocked ourselves** — arguments that wanted to go on the shelf but didn't hold up;
- **what we did not check** — stated plainly, so you don't mistake a gap for a finding.

**The document comes first, not last.** We don't read it to you and we don't defend it: you read
it yourself and you answer in writing — starting with **what you disagree with**. That is usually
where the real difference hides: an owner argues about the thing that is obvious to him and
invisible to his customer. Everything else — what you already knew, and what you do first —
gets settled in the same exchange.

> ### An example of a block, from this sample
>
> "We work with insurance companies" wanted to be the difference: it sounds substantial and it
> removes the money fear. **Blocked.** Three of five neighbors say it too, and what gets paid
> depends on the policy, not on the contractor. A promise that doesn't depend on you will be
> used against you in the conversation.

## What this document will not contain

- **Promises of results.** No percentage of growth, no number of leads, no payback date.
  That depends on your market and on what you do with the study.
- **A verdict on your workmanship.** We study how you are read, not how you lay a roof.
- **Someone else's numbers passed off as yours.** Every fact carries its source: the intake,
  open sources, or the owner's words.
