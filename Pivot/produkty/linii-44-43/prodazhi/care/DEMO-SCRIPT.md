# CareLine — 15-Minute Demo Script (owner of a NY home care agency)

*DRAFT 30.09.2026. For use only after the prototype passes acceptance (plan gate 09.10) and the demo number exists. Method: SPIN questions first (Rackham), then one Challenger reframe built on the owner's own numbers, then the live demo, then one concrete next step (an "Advance", never "let's stay in touch").*

**Hard rules for whoever runs this call**
- Say plainly that CareLine is an AI. Never say it "replaces" anyone, "never misses a call", "sounds human", or is "HIPAA / TCPA compliant".
- No result numbers, no savings math, no guarantees. If the owner does math, it's *his* math — write it down, don't repeat it back as our promise.
- Call-offs, family intake and EVV: always "this runs on demo data today; for your clients' data we sign a BAA first."
- We don't know what competitors can't do. Say "I haven't tested it."
- Ask before recording the Zoom call. (New York's recording rules weren't checked in our research — ask everyone, every time.)
- No customers yet. If asked, say so.

## Scene 1 — Opening (0:00–1:00)

> "Thanks for taking fifteen minutes, [Name]. Here's the plan: I'll ask you a few questions about nights and hiring, then show you the line live — you'll hear it yourself — and at the end we decide together whether a next step makes sense. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you. Fair?"

> "One thing up front: CareLine is an AI. When it answers, it tells callers it's an AI and that the call is recorded. Nothing I show you pretends to be a person."

> "Mind if I record this call so I don't have to take notes the whole time?" *(If no — don't record.)*

---

## Scene 2 — Discovery with SPIN (1:00–4:30)

Ask, then shut up. Write down his exact words — they become the recap email and the proposal.

**Situation (max 2 — we did our homework):**
- "Roughly how many aides are on your payroll right now?"
- "Who handles applicants and call-offs today — during the day, and after hours?"

**Problem (2–3):**
- "When an applicant calls your ad on a Saturday evening, what happens to that call?"
- "Walk me through the last time an aide called off before 6 AM. Who found out first?"
- "When does your office find EVV problems — before billing or after?"

**Implication (pick 3–4; these do the selling):**
- "And that Saturday applicant — by Monday, where are they?"
- "How many weekends in the last three months did you personally have the phone?"
- "When a 5 AM call-off doesn't get covered, what happens with the client — and with the plan?"
- "If your night person quit tomorrow, who takes the phone?"
- "When a visit gets flagged after billing, the aide is already paid — what do you do then?"
- "Is this getting better or worse since the CDPAP change and the talk about the RFO?"

**Need-payoff (1–2, only after implications):**
- "If Saturday applicants were screened and on your interview calendar by Monday morning, what would that change for you?"
- "If a 5 AM call-off reached a replacement before it reached you, what would you do with those mornings?"

> Listen for: **a named cost** ("I pay my coordinator overtime", "my VA", "I lose them to the agency down the street") and **an emotional word** ("exhausted", "every weekend", "my wife"). Both go into the recap.

**Qualification you must have by 4:30:**
- About 30–200 aides? Local decision-maker? Which calendar for interviews (the pilot works with Google Calendar)? Private pay vs. Medicaid mix?
- If hiring is truly not a problem and nights are covered → go to **B12 (no fit)**. Don't force the demo.

## Scene 3 — The reframe (4:30–5:30)

Pick the one that matches his biggest pain. Keep it to 60 seconds. The numbers are his; the facts are sourced.

**If hiring hurt most:**
> "Here's what I'm testing with agencies in Brooklyn and Queens. The usual explanation is that hiring is a supply problem — 'there are no aides.' But with turnover around 75% a year across home care, hiring never stops — you just said you hired [his number] in three months. So maybe part of it is a phone problem: the applicant who calls at 8 PM on Saturday talks to whoever answers first. You told me yours reach a person on [his answer]. That's the gap I want to show you."

**If call-offs hurt most:**
> "It's easy to see the 5 AM call-off as a scheduling problem. From what you told me, it's really a who-wakes-up problem: the list is in your head or your coordinator's phone, so one of you wakes up every time. The fix isn't a better spreadsheet — it's rules you already use, applied by something that doesn't sleep, and a person who confirms."

**If EVV/billing hurt most:**
> "The expensive visit isn't the one that never happened. It's the one that happened, got paid to the aide, and then gets rejected. North Carolina started rejecting home health claims without EVV data in October 2025; Missouri started re-processing mismatched claims this May and taking payments back. We haven't seen New York do that. The real question is what you'd find if you checked every visit against the authorization *before* you bill."

Then state the requirements before naming the product ("A New Way"):
> "So whatever fixes this has to do three things: answer when your office can't, follow *your* rules — not its own — and leave a person in charge of the decisions that matter. Let me show you one way to do that."

## Scene 4 — Live demo (5:30–11:00)

### 4A. Hiring line — the call (5:30–8:30) — *live, no BAA needed*
> "Call this number as if you saw our job ad. Pick English, Spanish or Russian. Be a difficult applicant if you want."

What he should notice (point to each on screen **after** the call, not during):
1. The first thing it said: that it's an AI and the call is recorded.
2. It asked **his** kind of questions — certificate, area, transport, schedule, languages — and asked work authorization only in the allowed wording.
3. It read the name, number and time back to confirm.
4. The interview is now on the demo calendar, a confirmation email went out, and the applicant card is in the dashboard.

> Interaction point: "How does this compare with what happened to the last applicant who called you on a weekend?"

**If he asks for a person during the call:** show that in office hours it transfers to a coordinator; after hours it takes a message and schedules a callback — it doesn't guess.

### 4B. Call-offs — the 5 AM scenario (8:30–10:00) — *demo data*
> "This part runs on demo data today. Before any of your clients' data goes in, we sign a BAA."

Trigger the armed scenario: a DEMO aide texts *"Sick, can't make my 8 AM tomorrow."*
1. The line finds her next shift by her phone number.
2. The rules engine filters and ranks replacements: skills, the client's language, distance by ZIP, free time, overtime limit, SMS consent, knows the client, reliability. **The AI doesn't pick — your rules do.**
3. A text goes to the top three: "Shift at 8:00 AM, [area]. Reply YES."
4. First YES gets the shift; the others get "shift taken". Two YESes can't both get it.
5. Coordinator notified.
Then the bad branch: nobody answers → close to start time the on-call coordinator's phone rings with a "press 1 to accept" screen, then the next number in the chain; the dashboard marks the shift "not covered".

> Interaction point: "Who would you want woken up — and how long before the shift?" *(Wave size, 15-minute wait and the 2-hour threshold are his settings.)*

### 4C. EVV check before billing (10:00–10:30) — *demo data*
> "Same rule: demo data today; BAA before your data."

Upload the demo export with seeded errors. Show the fix list: what's wrong, why it would be denied, how to fix it — missing clock-out, location vs. client address, duration vs. authorization, one aide in two places at once, authorization dates and codes. Say: "Rules find these; the AI only explains them in plain words."

> Interaction point: "Does your EVV system show you this before you bill, or after?" *(If "before, already" → don't sell A4. Say so.)*

### 4D. Dashboard, 7 AM summary, off switch (10:30–11:00)
- Morning email: applicants, bookings, call-offs, what's still open.
- Consent log: who agreed to recording and texts, when.
- Off switch: one command returns the line to the owner's phone; if our system is down, calls go to his phone. *(Say this only after it passed the acceptance test call.)*

Rule: acknowledge → ask what's behind it → answer briefly → show, don't argue → return to the script. If an objection is really "I don't see the problem as big enough", **go back to Implication questions** instead of defending the product (SPIN: objections are prevented, not handled).

## Scene 6 — Close (13:00–15:00)

**1) Check what's left:**
> "Before we talk next steps — is there anything you've seen that worries you, or anything I didn't show?"

**2) Summarize the benefits *he* named (his words, not ours):**
> "You told me three things: [Saturday applicants are gone by Monday], [you've had the phone most weekends], and [billing finds EVV problems after the fact]."

**3) Propose one Advance — a dated action, not "let's keep in touch":**

*Option A — pilot of the hiring line:*
> "Here's what I'd suggest: a pilot of the hiring line only. You fill in the 15-minute requirement sheet by [day], share the interview calendar, and put the number in your job ads. The line is live on [date]. On [date + 7 days] we look at the first week's summary together, and you decide. Terms: [PILOT TERMS]. Does [day] work for the sheet?"

*Option B — if another person must see it:*
> "Who else should see this before you decide — your ops director, your DPS? Let's put 15 minutes on [date] with them. I'll bring the same demo."

*Option C — if call-offs/EVV is the main interest:*
> "Those modules need a BAA first. Can you send me your attorney's contact so we start the BAA review this week? Meanwhile, the hiring line can start without it."

**Not acceptable outcomes:** "Sounds good, send me info" with no date; "I'll think about it" with no follow-up time. If you hear them, go back to B7.
