GEO for dental clinics: what it costs and what's included

Published prices, the 2026 market band around them, and the part most agency pages skip: what the money does not buy.

By Muhammed Veysel Erin — Founder, Citevio · Last updated 18 August 2026

Agency GEO for a small business runs $1,500 to $5,000 a month, according to WebFX's May 2026 cost guide. Citevio's three dental plans run from just below that band to just above it: $1,400, $2,900 and $5,500 a month, each with a one-time setup fee. What changes between them is how many patient questions get tracked, and how much off-site work is included.

How much does GEO for a dental clinic cost in 2026?

For an independent practice or a small group, roughly $1,000 to $8,000 a month, depending on scope. WebFX puts small-business generative engine optimization (getting a business named inside AI answers) at $1,500 to $5,000 a month. Digital Elevator's pricing guide places a monitor-and-maintain retainer at $1,000 to $2,500 and mid-market work at $2,000 to $8,000. Larger companies pay well above that, up to $25,000 and beyond.

Both guides were published in May 2026, and both are agency-authored, so read them as the going rate rather than an independent survey. They agree on the shape of the market: there is a tool tier, a template tier, and a tier where someone is doing the work.

What does "3,600 AI answers a month" actually mean?

30 questions × 4 engines × 30 days. The same set of patient-style questions is asked on ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews every day, and what you get back is the pattern rather than one lucky result. Authority raises it to 45 questions and 5,400 answers; Dominance to 60 questions and 7,200 answers, with competitor questions included.

Daily is the point, not the padding. Profound tested roughly 80,000 prompts per engine and found that about 40 to 60% of the domains cited in AI responses are different a month later. Ask once and you have a snapshot that may not survive the week. Ask the same question 30 times in a month and you can tell a real change from noise, and see which competitor keeps taking the slot.

Source: Profound, July 2025, roughly 80,000 prompts per engine.

The questions are yours, not a generic list. They are written around your city and the treatments you actually sell, agreed during onboarding and revised as your services change. Which checks run behind those questions, and what counts as being visible, is written up in how we measure AI visibility.

Does the arithmetic work for an independent practice?

A single Invisalign case typically runs $5,000 to $8,000; three to four veneers, $2,775 to $10,000 — national averages that move with your market and the case itself. Authority at $2,900 a month is $34,800 a year plus a one-time $3,500 setup. At $5,000 a case, that is roughly eight cases in the first year and about seven in the years after. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.

Source: Invisalign comprehensive treatment averages $5,108 nationally and typically runs $5,000–$8,000 (ASQ360/Synchrony 2023 cost study; GoodRx-compiled ranges); porcelain veneers run $925–$2,500 per tooth, with a $1,765 national average per veneer (CareCredit, Dental Veneers Cost & Financing) — the $2,775–$10,000 range above is three to four of them.

The reason the arithmetic is worth doing at all is that the baseline is low for almost everyone. SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index looked at nearly 350,000 locations across 2,751 brands and found 1.2% recommended by ChatGPT, 7.4% by Perplexity and 11% by Gemini, against 35.9% appearing in Google's local 3-pack. Being absent from AI answers is the normal condition right now, which is exactly why it is still cheap to fix.

Source: SOCi 2026 Local Visibility Index, nearly 350,000 locations across 2,751 brands, reported by Search Engine Land.

Why publish a price at all?

Because "contact us for a quote" gives an AI assistant nothing to quote. When a clinic owner asks an assistant what this kind of work costs, the pages that answer with a number are the ones that can be repeated back. That is our own reading of how these engines handle buying questions, and this page is us acting on it.

The second reason is the one that binds us. A published price is a commitment you can be held to. It makes a like-for-like comparison possible without a sales call, and it means the number you see is the number, not an opening position that depends on how your practice looks on paper. If the plans change, the home page is the list that gets updated first: current pricing lives there.

If you stop paying, what stops and what stays?

What was changed on your website is yours and stays where it is: the pages, the structured data, the profile corrections, the question set and the raw engine answers collected along the way. What stops is the measurement and the work that follows it. In plain terms, you stop finding out what is happening rather than losing something you own.

The billing side of leaving is answered in the contract questions further down this page — no lock-in on monthly plans, and what happens to an annual term or the setup fee. This section is about the other half of that question, which contracts do not cover: what happens to the visibility itself.

Here is the honest answer, and it is shorter than a sales page would like. We do not know. Citevio has never tracked a practice's engine visibility after an engagement ended, because that measurement has not been run. Anyone telling you that your visibility decays on a particular curve after you stop is describing a fear, not a finding, and the correct response is to ask them for the study.

What can be said comes from measuring the answers while the work is still running. Across three fixed queries run twice on each of two engines through their APIs on the evening of 15 August 2026, 12 of 12 calls completed, and the displayed order moved between runs — on one engine the same query returned first place in one run and third in the next, which is one evening's photograph rather than a trend. If the order moves that much inside a single evening with the work in progress, no one is in a position to publish a decay rate for what happens after it stops.

Source: Citevio fixed-query run, 15 August 2026, 12 of 12 API calls completed. API responses are not the consumer apps, and the two engines are reported separately, never pooled.

That volatility is the general condition rather than something particular to us, and it is written up with the sampling behind it in how often do AI answers change. What we count as a real move rather than noise is on how we measure AI visibility. Both matter more at the point of leaving than at the point of signing, which is why they are linked here rather than only in the methodology section.

How do you prove an AI citation turned into a patient?

AI answers increasingly resolve without a click — that's exactly why Layer 5 of the protocol measures what happens after the answer, not the click itself. It adds a source field and an "AI assistant" option to the booking form, a call-tracking template, and an "AI-sourced" line in the monthly report; the appointment data stays in the practice's own systems, only the monthly count reaches us. This gets installed with a practice's actual booking form at onboarding — until then, it's a design commitment, not a delivered number.

Might this budget work better somewhere else?

Sometimes, yes. If your consult no-show rate is high, or your ads aren't tracked, fix that first — AI visibility won't fix a leak somewhere else in the funnel.
